ER DIAGRAM TO RELATIONAL SCHEMA TRANSFORMATION
Synapse Fest Database

================================================================
OVERVIEW
================================================================
The Synapse Fest Database was initially modeled using an
Entity-Relationship (ER) diagram representing the major entities,
attributes, and relationships involved in managing a college festival.
The ER diagram was then transformed into a relational schema by applying
standard ER-to-Relational mapping rules.

The objective was to convert conceptual entities and relationships into
relations that can be implemented in PostgreSQL while preserving data
integrity and minimizing redundancy.

================================================================
MAPPING RULES
================================================================
1. Every strong entity was converted into a relation with its primary
   key.
2. Multivalued attributes (e.g. phone numbers) were separated into
   independent relations.
3. One-to-Many (1:N) relationships were implemented using foreign keys
   on the "many" side.
4. Many-to-Many (M:N) relationships were converted into associative
   relations containing the primary keys of the participating entities.
5. Derived attributes were not stored; they are computed whenever
   required.
6. Integrity constraints were enforced through primary keys, foreign
   keys, CHECK constraints, and triggers.

================================================================
1NF: MULTIVALUED ATTRIBUTES
================================================================
Several entities contained multivalued phone numbers in the ER model.
To maintain First Normal Form (1NF), separate relations were created:

  COMMITTEE_PHONE(comm_id, phone)
  ATTENDEE_PHONE(attendee_id, phone)
  ARTIST_PHONE(artist_id, phone)
  JUDGES_PHONE(judge_id, phone)
  VOLUNTEER_PHONE(volunteer_id, phone)
  SPONSOR_PHONE(sponsor_id, phone)
  VENDOR_PHONE(vendor_id, phone)

Each relation uses a composite primary key consisting of the owner
identifier and phone number.

================================================================
RELATIONSHIP MAPPING
================================================================

One-to-Many Relationships
----------------------------------------------------------------
Relationship                                Implementation
----------------------------------------------------------------
Committee manages Event                     EVENT.comm_id
Venue hosts Event                           EVENT.venue_id
Venue contains Vendor                       VENDOR.venue_id
Venue associated with Lost & Found report   LOST_FOUND.venue_id
Attendee purchases Ticket                   TICKET.attendee_id
Ticket belongs to Ticket Type                TICKET.ticket_type_id

Many-to-Many Relationships
----------------------------------------------------------------
Relationship                    Associative Relation
----------------------------------------------------------------
Event -- Judge                  EVENT_JUDGES
Event -- Artist                 EVENT_ARTIST
Ticket Type -- Event            TICKET_TYPE_EVENT
Committee -- Sponsor            COMMITTEE_SPONSOR
Committee -- Volunteer          COMMITTEE_VOLUNTEER
Committee -- Committee Member   COMMITTEE_COMMITTEE_MEMBER
Venue -- Facility               VENUE_FACILITY

================================================================
TABLES (pre-normalization, derived directly from the ER model)
================================================================

EVENT(event_id, event_name, category, rules, prize_money, date,
      start_time, end_time, cost_event, venue_id, comm_id)

JUDGES(judge_id, first_name, middle_name, last_name, email, fee, genre)
JUDGES_PHONE(judge_id, phone)
EVENT_JUDGES(event_id, judge_id)

ARTIST(artist_id, first_name, middle_name, last_name, email, fee, genre)
ARTIST_PHONE(artist_id, phone)
EVENT_ARTIST(event_id, artist_id)

VENUE(venue_id, venue_name, capacity, location)
VENUE_FACILITY(venue_id, facility)

COMMITTEE(comm_id, comm_name, email)
COMMITTEE_PHONE(comm_id, phone)

TICKET_TYPE(ticket_type_id, ticket_type_name, price)
TICKET_TYPE_EVENT(ticket_type_id, event_id)
TICKET(ticket_id, purchase_time, status, attendee_id, ticket_type_id)

ATTENDEE(attendee_id, first_name, middle_name, last_name, gender,
         category, email, dob)                 -- student, professor, etc.
ATTENDEE_PHONE(attendee_id, phone)

ACCOMMODATION(attendee_id, room_id, check_in_date_time, check_out_date,
              number_of_guests, amount, status)
HOSTEL_ROOM(room_id, hostel_name, capacity, status, rate_per_night)

VENDOR(vendor_id, venue_id, stall_name, first_name, middle_name,
       last_name, email, service_type, rent_per_day)
VENDOR_PHONE(vendor_id, phone)

LOST_FOUND(item_id, query(lost/found), description, status, venue_id)
LOST_FOUND_PHONE(item_id, phone)

BUDGET(budget_id, comm_id, amount, status)

SPONSOR(sponsor_id, sponsor_name, email)
COMMITTEE_SPONSOR(comm_id, sponsor_id, amount, status)
SPONSOR_PHONE(sponsor_id, phone)

COMMITTEE_MEMBER(member_id, first_name, middle_name, last_name, email,
                  position, year_experience)
COMMITTEE_MEMBER_PHONE(member_id, phone)
COMMITTEE_COMMITTEE_MEMBER(comm_id, member_id)

VOLUNTEER(volunteer_id, first_name, middle_name, last_name, email)
VOLUNTEER_PHONE(volunteer_id, phone)
COMMITTEE_VOLUNTEER(comm_id, volunteer_id)

FEEDBACK(feedback_id, feedback, reviewer_type, rating (out of 5), date)

Note: this list reflects the schema exactly as derived from the ER
diagram, before normalization is applied. See Normalization.txt for the
2NF/3NF/BCNF analysis and the final decomposed schema, and
create_tables.txt for the implemented PostgreSQL DDL.
